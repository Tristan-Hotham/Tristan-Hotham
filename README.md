### Hi, I'm Dr Tristan Hotham

**Data-led researcher of politics, the past, platforms and places. Maker of maps, games and other things.**

I take public data, the messier the better, and turn it into interactive maps, papers and things you can play with: the past, places and weather in Britain and around the world, and how political parties, leaders and campaigns use Facebook and other platforms.

#### 🗺️ Data stories: the past, places, politics and weather

Questions about the past, places, politics, everyday life and the weather, in Britain and around the world, answered with open data. Each one has an interactive page, a [blog](https://blog.tristanhotham.com/) write-up, and data and code here on GitHub, most with an archived paper on Zenodo. The [data stories page](https://tristanhotham.com/data/) shows them all as cards.

**People and politics**

- [Born Here](https://maps.tristanhotham.com/famous-births/): 166,586 notable people matched to the UK towns they were born in: the most famous person from each, the towns that punch above their weight, and why so many are hospital towns.
- [Born and Bred? How Local Are MPs?](https://maps.tristanhotham.com/mp-localness/): Where 10,094 MPs since 1832 were born, set against the seats they won: for 170 years about a quarter were born in their seat's county, and since 2010 it is 41.9%.
- [Who Is Your Town Twinned With?](https://maps.tristanhotham.com/twin-towns/): All 29,437 twin town and sister city links on Wikidata as arcs on one map: a post-war European habit that peaked in 1990 and lost dozens of Russian links after 2022.
- [Mentioned in the House](https://maps.tristanhotham.com/hansard-places/): 297,134 Commons mentions of UK towns and cities in Hansard since 1909: which places punch above their weight, and who names your constituency.
- [Who Won Here?](https://maps.tristanhotham.com/elections-since-1832/): All 49 UK general elections from 1832 to 2024 on one map: 31,736 seats, the forgotten uncontested elections, the Liberal fall and the Red Wall.
- [School Catchments](https://maps.tristanhotham.com/school-catchments/): Every one of the UK's 27,024 state schools and how much ground each serves: the biggest catchment, Golspie High School, covers 2,215 km².
- [Where Would You Go to University?](https://maps.tristanhotham.com/study-abroad/): Every country by where its students most often go to study abroad, 1999 to 2023: old empires still show, Turkey is a magnet, and India has overtaken China in Britain.

**Everyday life**

- [What Does the World Drink?](https://maps.tristanhotham.com/drinks-map/): 1,985 protected drink names, 32,425 wineries, breweries and distilleries and the national drink of 201 countries: the national drink is often tea.
- [What Does the World Eat?](https://maps.tristanhotham.com/food-map/): National dishes for 186 countries, breakfasts for 73 and 2,706 regional dishes from fabada in Asturias to takoyaki in Osaka, and why there is no lunch map.
- [Chips, Curry or Kebab?](https://maps.tristanhotham.com/takeaways/): 62,949 takeaways and 141,306 restaurants on the food hygiene register sorted by what their names sell: the chip shop capital is the seaside, the curry capital Hounslow.
- [Signature Sports](https://maps.tristanhotham.com/niche-sports/): Which sport each country plays far more than the world, from 99,246 clubs: pesäpallo in Finland, tchoukball in Switzerland, hurling in Ireland, speedway in Poland.
- [Born Here, Loved There](https://maps.tristanhotham.com/dog-breeds/): 573 dog breeds mapped to their countries of origin and set against kennel club registrations: in 8 of 10 countries the favourite dog was born somewhere else.
- [What Britain Drives](https://maps.tristanhotham.com/cars/): 42,728,066 MOT tests mapped by make, model and colour: the average British car is a blue Ford Fiesta, Scotland prefers white, and Shetland loves Toyotas.

**Names on the map**

- [What Pub Names Remember](https://maps.tristanhotham.com/pub-sign-history/): Which histories do 37,559 pub signs carry, and which names give a county away?
- [The Geography of Saints](https://maps.tristanhotham.com/saints-geography/): Where does each saint's following lie across 17,116 British church dedications?
- [Whose Church Is It?](https://maps.tristanhotham.com/saints-and-wells/): 279,212 churches in twelve Western European countries, sorted by patron saint.
- [From Saint to Wish](https://maps.tristanhotham.com/holy-wells/): 3,440 holy and wishing wells in Britain and Ireland: their saints, languages, cures and offerings.
- [Is There a Barrow in Barrow?](https://maps.tristanhotham.com/barrow-in-barrow/): Do places named from Old English beorg sit by burial mounds?
- [Dale, Down, Fell and Beck](https://maps.tristanhotham.com/dale-down-fell-beck/): Where do Britain's words for hills, valleys and streams change?
- [Ford or Bridge?](https://maps.tristanhotham.com/fords-and-bridges/): Every ford and bridge place name in Britain against the census and income: Oxford waded, Cambridge built.
- [Bloody Place Names](https://maps.tristanhotham.com/bloody-place-names/): 756 grim names on the OS map against 507 battles: only the Battles mark battlefields.
- [Gallows Hill](https://maps.tristanhotham.com/gallows-hill/): Where Britain put its gallows: 340 names measured for height, roads, towns and boundaries.
- [Silver and Names](https://maps.tristanhotham.com/viking-silver/): Viking hoards against Norse place names: they mostly agree, except in Lincolnshire.
- [Here Be Dragons](https://maps.tristanhotham.com/here-be-dragons/): Britain's dragon legends and place names: they cling to the parish church, not the barrow or the river.
- [The High Ones](https://maps.tristanhotham.com/the-high-ones/): What links the Brigantes of Yorkshire and Brigantium in Galicia?
- [Barm, Bap, Cob or Roll?](https://maps.tristanhotham.com/bread-rolls/): 20,038 dialect survey answers mapped into Britain's bread roll lines, checked against the high street.
- [Where the Devil Lives](https://maps.tristanhotham.com/devils-britain/): 107 Devil place names in Britain: 31% lie within 1 km of a prehistoric monument, against 19% of comparable places.
- [Haunted, or Just Old?](https://maps.tristanhotham.com/ghosts/): 260 reportedly haunted places against 40,409 top listed buildings: castles and inns are 9 times as common among them, churches almost vanish.
- [Giants in the Landscape](https://maps.tristanhotham.com/giants/): 331 giant places in Britain and Ireland: giants own graves, not mountains.
- [Has Your Surname Left Home?](https://maps.tristanhotham.com/surname-birthplaces/): Where 133,761 notable Britons with each surname were born, 1800s against since 1950: Welsh Joneses and Cornish Tre- names are leaving home, though slowly.
- [-ton, -by, Llan- and Bally-](https://maps.tristanhotham.com/place-name-endings/): 105,532 place names in Britain and Ireland tagged by the English, Norse, Latin, Welsh and Gaelic words inside them: Kil- is a wood far more often than you might think.
- [High Street or Main Street?](https://maps.tristanhotham.com/street-names/): All 913,335 named streets in the UK sorted by name: High Street rules the towns, Main Street the villages of Scotland, Northern Ireland and parts of England.
- [Games on the Map](https://maps.tristanhotham.com/games-on-the-map/): A world map of 6,691 video games tied to real places: New York leads, Japan to the United States is the busiest route from studio to setting, and the Moon beats Mars.
- [What the World Calls Each Other](https://maps.tristanhotham.com/country-names/): 54,037 country names in 262 languages: most countries keep something like their own name everywhere, but Germany has six families of names.

**Prehistory and monuments**

- [Same Shape, Different Millennia](https://maps.tristanhotham.com/stone-circles/): Every stone circle in Europe's open data, sorted into traditions and dated.
- [The Megalith Highway](https://maps.tristanhotham.com/megalith-highway/): 13,280 megalithic monuments: did the idea of the great stone tomb travel by sea?
- [Is the Barrow a European Thing?](https://maps.tristanhotham.com/barrows-of-europe/): 99,049 burial mounds from Ireland to the steppe, with radiocarbon dates.
- [Rings in the Rock](https://maps.tristanhotham.com/rings-in-the-rock/): Where are Atlantic Europe's cup-and-ring carvings, and who carved them?
- [Within Sight](https://maps.tristanhotham.com/within-sight/): Could Britain and Ireland's hillforts see each other?
- [The Famous Few](https://maps.tristanhotham.com/famous-few/): Why do eleven hillforts get half of all Wikipedia reading?
- [The Triskell Boom](https://maps.tristanhotham.com/triskell-boom/): How did an ancient triple spiral become a modern Celtic badge?
- [Still Standing](https://maps.tristanhotham.com/oldest-buildings/): 6,837 buildings from before AD 1100 on one map, and what kept them standing.
- [Did Detectorists Start a Boom?](https://maps.tristanhotham.com/detectorists-effect/): Did a sitcom send Britain out metal detecting?
- [Ley Lines, Tested](https://maps.tristanhotham.com/ley-lines/): 50,822 churches, barrows, stones, hillforts and wells against random points: crowding, not planning, explains the leys.
- [Did Fairy Belief Protect Ireland's Ringforts?](https://maps.tristanhotham.com/fairy-forts/): 31,431 ringforts set against barrows, enclosures and moated sites: the fairies left few fingerprints.

**Romans to Tudors**

- [How Roman Is Your Road?](https://maps.tristanhotham.com/how-roman-is-your-road/): How much of each county's A-roads still follows a Roman line?
- [The Roman Commuter Belt?](https://maps.tristanhotham.com/roman-villas/): 617 Roman villas against today's household income: a premium that is almost all geography.
- [Minted](https://maps.tristanhotham.com/saxon-mints/): England's Anglo-Saxon and Norman mint towns from 70,995 coins: were they still the big towns in 1377, 1801 and today?
- [Is Domesday Destiny?](https://maps.tristanhotham.com/domesday-destiny/): Does a place's size in 1086 still predict its size today?
- [Laid Waste: the Harrying of the North](https://maps.tristanhotham.com/harrying-of-the-north/): Where Domesday's 'waste' fell after 1069 to 1070, and how faint its scar is today.
- [The Conquest in Castles](https://maps.tristanhotham.com/norman-castles/): How fast Norman castles spread across Britain after Hastings, decade by decade.
- [The Villages That Vanished](https://maps.tristanhotham.com/lost-villages/): England's 2,222 deserted medieval villages against Domesday Book.
- [Where Was the Battle?](https://maps.tristanhotham.com/where-was-the-battle/): 88 proposed sites for 18 lost battles of early Britain, scored against the texts.
- [What Became of the Abbeys?](https://maps.tristanhotham.com/after-the-abbeys/): 808 houses Henry VIII closed, traced to what stands on their sites today.
- [Governing on the Move](https://maps.tristanhotham.com/royal-itineraries/): King John and Edward I mapped night by night: John moved on 42% of the days we can follow him, Edward on 23%.
- [Market Towns That Failed](https://maps.tristanhotham.com/medieval-markets/): All 2,173 medieval market places in England and Wales, followed to today: 665 are towns, 1,168 villages, and a few are fields or sea.
- [From Domesday Mills to Modern Mills](https://maps.tristanhotham.com/domesday-mills/): 6,172 mills in Domesday Book: places that had one are 3.9 times as likely to have a watermill nearby today, but not a windmill.
- [Britain in AD 500](https://maps.tristanhotham.com/britain-500/): What the ground can place instead of neat kingdoms: 891 pieces of evidence, a sharp east of graves and west of stones, and 24 kingdoms we can name but not draw.

**Early modern and modern**

- [Fire on the Hills](https://maps.tristanhotham.com/armada-beacons/): Could the Armada beacons carry the news? 193 beacon hills and the lines of sight between them.
- [Cold Years, Witch Years?](https://maps.tristanhotham.com/witch-hunts/): Scotland's 3,840 accused witches: did the panics follow cold years? They did not.
- [Canal Mania](https://maps.tristanhotham.com/canal-mania/): Did England's canal towns boom, and do they still lead? Bigger today, not richer.
- [Cholera 1849](https://maps.tristanhotham.com/cholera-1849/): Farr's elevation law, Snow's pump and whether London's worst-hit districts are still the poorest.
- [Rotten Boroughs: Where Are They Now?](https://maps.tristanhotham.com/rotten-boroughs/): The 56 boroughs the 1832 Reform Act abolished, followed to today: 25 are villages, a hamlet or an empty hill.
- [Did Lighthouses Work?](https://maps.tristanhotham.com/did-lighthouses-work/): Did shipwrecks fall after the lights were lit?
- [Whose Ships Sank Where?](https://maps.tristanhotham.com/wrecks-of-europe/): 106,550 shipwrecks in Europe's seas, 1744 to 1945, by the flag they sailed under.
- [Thankful Villages: Luck or Size?](https://maps.tristanhotham.com/thankful-villages/): Why did every man come home to some First World War parishes?
- [Far From Home](https://maps.tristanhotham.com/victoria-cross/): Where each Victoria Cross was won, where its recipient was born, and the distance between.
- [There and Back Again](https://maps.tristanhotham.com/there-and-back-again/): 43 great journeys from 600 BC to 1947, mapped and graded by the evidence that they happened.
- [The Turnpike Towns](https://maps.tristanhotham.com/turnpike-towns/): England's 1,245 turnpike trusts and 10,927 surviving milestones and toll houses: did the towns on the turnpikes grow faster? Not that the data show.
- [What's the King on Your Postbox?](https://maps.tristanhotham.com/postbox-kings/): The royal cypher on 61,435 UK post boxes: 3,614 still carry Victoria's VR and 143 Edward VIII's.
- [The Lido Boom and Bust](https://maps.tristanhotham.com/lido-boom/): An open register of 73 British lidos: 33 of 49 dated openings came in the 1930s, and 14 closed pools have reopened.
- [Did the Pits Closing Still Show?](https://maps.tristanhotham.com/pit-closures/): 167 of 196 deep mines closed between 1984 and 1994. Forty years on, pit towns have 2.0 points more adults long-term sick than similar towns.
- [Bombs on Britain](https://maps.tristanhotham.com/air-raids/): All 61,542 civilians on the CWGC's Second World War roll for the UK, town by town: 51% died outside London.
- [Ring the Changes](https://maps.tristanhotham.com/ring-the-changes/): All 7,262 rings of change-ringing bells in Dove's Guide, mapped: jubilees, world wars and seven centuries of bell founding.
- [How Many Names on Your War Memorial?](https://maps.tristanhotham.com/war-memorials/): First World War names on 14,119 community memorials: 18.3 for every 1,000 people living there today, and only 36 small places record no dead.
- [Did the Railway Invent the Seaside?](https://maps.tristanhotham.com/seaside-railways/): Britain's resorts were already growing 1.6% a year before the railway came. The line brought a burst in its first decade.
- [Captain Swing](https://maps.tristanhotham.com/captain-swing/): 3,117 Swing riot incidents of 1830: parishes with a threshing machine were twice as likely to riot, but 90% of riotous parishes had none.
- [Britain's Last Line of Defence](https://maps.tristanhotham.com/stop-lines/): 14,690 pillboxes and defences of 1940: the planned German landing beaches had 2.3 times the average density.
- [Did Beeching Kill Your Town?](https://maps.tristanhotham.com/beeching-cuts/): 3,331 stations closed from 1955 to 1975, and 643 towns lost every one. They are a little poorer and more car-bound today, but did not fall off a cliff.
- [Great Fires](https://maps.tristanhotham.com/great-fires/): 102 great town fires, 63% of them in the 1600s and 1700s: did the towns that burned rebuild bigger? Not clearly.
- [Who Gets a Blue Plaque?](https://maps.tristanhotham.com/blue-plaques/): 6,476 plaques across the UK and all 1,037 English Heritage plaques in London: 16% honour women, but 54% of those since 2020.
- [The Listed Kingdom](https://maps.tristanhotham.com/listed-buildings/): All 465,740 entries on the UK's four lists of listed buildings on one map: mostly Georgian and Victorian houses, with a great many churches at the top.

**Land, sea and sky**

- [The Footpath Capital](https://maps.tristanhotham.com/footpaths/): Every council in England and Wales by miles of public footpath per square mile: Rossendale tops it at 7.7, and the South Pennine mill towns fill the top ten.
- [Drowned and Rising](https://maps.tristanhotham.com/sea-level/): A bathtub model of every sea level from the Ice Age to an ice-free world: Britain becomes an island at −30 m, and at +70 m a third of the UK is under water.
- [How Many Months Are Pleasantly Warm?](https://maps.tristanhotham.com/pleasant-months/): Months with afternoon highs of 18 to 27 °C: the average city dweller gets 3.6 a year now and 2.9 by 2041 to 2060. Pick your own band.
- [Sun and Rain: the World](https://maps.tristanhotham.com/sun-and-rain/): The world's sunshine against its rainfall: only 3% of land is both sunny and wet, and 84% is sunnier than anywhere in the UK.
- [Sun and Rain: the UK](https://maps.tristanhotham.com/sun-and-rain-uk/): Every square kilometre of the UK by sunshine and rainfall at once. Since 1961 to 1990 the country has become 5.6% sunnier and 7.2% wetter.
- [When Did Your Town Last See a Total Eclipse?](https://maps.tristanhotham.com/eclipse-towns/): Every path of totality across Britain and Ireland from AD 1000 to 2200, tested for 17,103 places: for most towns the last was in 1715 or 1724.
- [Britain's Earthquakes Since 1382](https://maps.tristanhotham.com/british-earthquakes/): All 7,032 quakes in the BGS catalogues: a magnitude 4 about every 3 years, and the busiest modern zones were coalfields.
- [Where Are Britain's Oldest Trees?](https://maps.tristanhotham.com/ancient-trees/): 1,626 notable trees on OpenStreetMap: oaks dominate, yews hug the old churches, and giants stand in the royal forests.
- [Signature Species](https://maps.tristanhotham.com/county-wildlife/): 60.9 million open wildlife records sorted into 118 vice counties, to find the species each one records far more often than the rest of Britain and Northern Ireland.
- [Darkest Skies](https://maps.tristanhotham.com/dark-skies/): The glow of the world's lights modelled from satellite images and checked against 6,653 sky meter readings: 52% of people, and 85% in the UK, cannot see the Milky Way from home.
- [Where Rain Goes](https://maps.tristanhotham.com/where-rain-goes/): Every place in Britain, Ireland and the world followed downhill to the sea: 50% of Great Britain drains to the North Sea, and 24% of the world's land never reaches the sea at all.
- [How Wrong Is Your Clock?](https://maps.tristanhotham.com/clock-vs-sun/): For 76% of the world's people the clock runs ahead of the sun: the gap everywhere, and why winter mornings in Galicia stay dark until after nine.
- [What Grows Where](https://maps.tristanhotham.com/tree-map/): Every wood in Great Britain by type, England's public forests by species and planting year, and 88 tree genera worldwide: woodland covers 13.7% of Great Britain, 19.1% of Scotland but 10.3% of England.
- [Hard Water](https://maps.tristanhotham.com/hard-water/): Water hardness from 16,682 river, lake and groundwater stations, and a simple model that guesses it from the rocks: Scandinavia's median is 14 mg/l, the Yucatán's 514.
- [The Rocks Beneath](https://maps.tristanhotham.com/rocks-beneath/): A geological map of the British Isles and the world in the colours of 1969: Carboniferous rocks cover 17.5% of Great Britain, and England's layers get younger all the way to London.

#### 📊 Research: politics and platforms

My PhD (University of Bath, 2021) looked at how British political parties use Facebook in election campaigns. I work with digital trace data (CrowdTangle, Netvizz, the Meta Ad Library), hand coding and text-as-data methods. Where the terms of collection allow, I share aggregate data with each paper.

- Party and leader pages, and who carries a party's online audience
- Satellite campaigns such as Momentum, and Janus-faced campaigning
- Targeted political advertising and A/B testing
- Comment sections, incivility and toxicity
- Populism, the radical right and engagement across Europe

Selected papers (open access, with DOIs, PDFs and aggregate data):

- [Do Populists Win on Facebook? The Engagement Premium of Populist Parties in Europe, 2010 to 2020](https://tristanhotham.com/research/do-populists-win-on-facebook/)
- [What Does a Satellite Campaign Do? Momentum, the Labour Party and Jeremy Corbyn on Facebook in the 2017 General Election](https://tristanhotham.com/research/what-does-a-satellite-campaign-do/)
- [Same Message, Thousands of Times: Targeted Facebook Advertising by British Parties at the 2017 and 2019 General Elections](https://tristanhotham.com/research/same-message-thousands-of-times/)
- [Who Carries the Party on Facebook? Leaders, Party Pages and the Polls in Britain, 2010 to 2020](https://tristanhotham.com/research/who-carries-the-party-on-facebook/)
