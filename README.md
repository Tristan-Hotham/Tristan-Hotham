### Hi, I'm Dr Tristan Hotham

**Researcher of campaigns and platforms. Maker of other things.**

I take public data, the messier the better, and turn it into papers, interactive maps and things you can play with. Most of my research is about how political parties, leaders and campaigns use Facebook and other platforms. Lately I have pointed the same methods at the past: hillforts, Roman roads, pub signs, saints, shipwrecks, witch trials and the Domesday Book.

#### 📊 Research: platforms and politics

My PhD (University of Bath, 2021) looked at how British political parties use Facebook in election campaigns. I work with digital trace data (CrowdTangle, Netvizz, the Meta Ad Library), hand coding and text-as-data methods. Where the terms of collection allow, I share aggregate data with each paper.

- Party and leader pages, and who carries a party's online audience
- Satellite campaigns such as Momentum, and Janus-faced campaigning
- Targeted political advertising and A/B testing
- Comment sections, incivility and toxicity
- Populism, the radical right and engagement across Europe

Selected papers (open access, with DOIs, PDFs and aggregate data):

#### 🗺️ Data studies: history, places and landscape

69 questions about the past, the landscape and the weather, answered with open data. Each one has an interactive page, a [blog](https://blog.tristanhotham.com/) write-up, and data and code here on GitHub, most with an archived paper on Zenodo. The [hub](https://tristan-hotham.github.io/) shows them all as cards.

**Names on the map**

- [What Pub Names Remember](https://tristan-hotham.github.io/pub-sign-history/): Which histories do 37,559 pub signs carry, and which names give a county away?
- [The Geography of Saints](https://tristan-hotham.github.io/saints-geography/): Where does each saint's following lie across 17,116 British church dedications?
- [Whose Church Is It?](https://tristan-hotham.github.io/saints-and-wells/): 279,212 churches in twelve Western European countries, sorted by patron saint.
- [From Saint to Wish](https://tristan-hotham.github.io/holy-wells/): 3,440 holy and wishing wells in Britain and Ireland: their saints, languages, cures and offerings.
- [Is There a Barrow in Barrow?](https://tristan-hotham.github.io/barrow-in-barrow/): Do places named from Old English beorg sit by burial mounds?
- [Dale, Down, Fell and Beck](https://tristan-hotham.github.io/dale-down-fell-beck/): Where do Britain's words for hills, valleys and streams change?
- [Ford or Bridge?](https://tristan-hotham.github.io/fords-and-bridges/): Every ford and bridge place name in Britain against the census and income: Oxford waded, Cambridge built.
- [Bloody Place Names](https://tristan-hotham.github.io/bloody-place-names/): 756 grim names on the OS map against 507 battles: only the Battles mark battlefields.
- [Gallows Hill](https://tristan-hotham.github.io/gallows-hill/): Where Britain put its gallows: 340 names measured for height, roads, towns and boundaries.
- [Silver and Names](https://tristan-hotham.github.io/viking-silver/): Viking hoards against Norse place names: they mostly agree, except in Lincolnshire.
- [Here Be Dragons](https://tristan-hotham.github.io/here-be-dragons/): Britain's dragon legends and place names: they cling to the parish church, not the barrow or the river.
- [The High Ones](https://tristan-hotham.github.io/the-high-ones/): What links the Brigantes of Yorkshire and Brigantium in Galicia?
- [Barm, Bap, Cob or Roll?](https://tristan-hotham.github.io/bread-rolls/): 20,038 dialect survey answers mapped into Britain's bread roll lines, checked against the high street.
- [Where the Devil Lives](https://tristan-hotham.github.io/devils-britain/): 107 Devil place names in Britain: 31% lie within 1 km of a prehistoric monument, against 19% of comparable places.
- [Haunted, or Just Old?](https://tristan-hotham.github.io/ghosts/): 260 reportedly haunted places against 40,409 top listed buildings: castles and inns are 9 times as common among them, churches almost vanish.
- [Giants in the Landscape](https://tristan-hotham.github.io/giants/): 331 giant places in Britain and Ireland: giants own graves, not mountains.

**Prehistory and monuments**

- [Same Shape, Different Millennia](https://tristan-hotham.github.io/stone-circles/): Every stone circle in Europe's open data, sorted into traditions and dated.
- [The Megalith Highway](https://tristan-hotham.github.io/megalith-highway/): 13,280 megalithic monuments: did the idea of the great stone tomb travel by sea?
- [Is the Barrow a European Thing?](https://tristan-hotham.github.io/barrows-of-europe/): 99,049 burial mounds from Ireland to the steppe, with radiocarbon dates.
- [Rings in the Rock](https://tristan-hotham.github.io/rings-in-the-rock/): Where are Atlantic Europe's cup-and-ring carvings, and who carved them?
- [Within Sight](https://tristan-hotham.github.io/within-sight/): Could Britain and Ireland's hillforts see each other?
- [The Famous Few](https://tristan-hotham.github.io/famous-few/): Why do eleven hillforts get half of all Wikipedia reading?
- [The Triskell Boom](https://tristan-hotham.github.io/triskell-boom/): How did an ancient triple spiral become a modern Celtic badge?
- [Still Standing](https://tristan-hotham.github.io/oldest-buildings/): 6,837 buildings from before AD 1100 on one map, and what kept them standing.
- [Did Detectorists Start a Boom?](https://tristan-hotham.github.io/detectorists-effect/): Did a sitcom send Britain out metal detecting?
- [Ley Lines, Tested](https://tristan-hotham.github.io/ley-lines/): 50,822 churches, barrows, stones, hillforts and wells against random points: crowding, not planning, explains the leys.
- [Did Fairy Belief Protect Ireland's Ringforts?](https://tristan-hotham.github.io/fairy-forts/): 31,431 ringforts set against barrows, enclosures and moated sites: the fairies left few fingerprints.

**Romans to Tudors**

- [How Roman Is Your Road?](https://tristan-hotham.github.io/how-roman-is-your-road/): How much of each county's A-roads still follows a Roman line?
- [The Roman Commuter Belt?](https://tristan-hotham.github.io/roman-villas/): 617 Roman villas against today's household income: a premium that is almost all geography.
- [Minted](https://tristan-hotham.github.io/saxon-mints/): England's Anglo-Saxon and Norman mint towns from 70,995 coins: were they still the big towns in 1377, 1801 and today?
- [Is Domesday Destiny?](https://tristan-hotham.github.io/domesday-destiny/): Does a place's size in 1086 still predict its size today?
- [Laid Waste: the Harrying of the North](https://tristan-hotham.github.io/harrying-of-the-north/): Where Domesday's 'waste' fell after 1069 to 1070, and how faint its scar is today.
- [The Conquest in Castles](https://tristan-hotham.github.io/norman-castles/): How fast Norman castles spread across Britain after Hastings, decade by decade.
- [The Villages That Vanished](https://tristan-hotham.github.io/lost-villages/): England's 2,222 deserted medieval villages against Domesday Book.
- [Where Was the Battle?](https://tristan-hotham.github.io/where-was-the-battle/): 88 proposed sites for 18 lost battles of early Britain, scored against the texts.
- [What Became of the Abbeys?](https://tristan-hotham.github.io/after-the-abbeys/): 808 houses Henry VIII closed, traced to what stands on their sites today.
- [Governing on the Move](https://tristan-hotham.github.io/royal-itineraries/): King John and Edward I mapped night by night: John moved on 42% of the days we can follow him, Edward on 23%.
- [Market Towns That Failed](https://tristan-hotham.github.io/medieval-markets/): All 2,173 medieval market places in England and Wales, followed to today: 665 are towns, 1,168 villages, and a few are fields or sea.
- [From Domesday Mills to Modern Mills](https://tristan-hotham.github.io/domesday-mills/): 6,172 mills in Domesday Book: places that had one are 3.9 times as likely to have a watermill nearby today, but not a windmill.

**Early modern and modern**

- [Fire on the Hills](https://tristan-hotham.github.io/armada-beacons/): Could the Armada beacons carry the news? 193 beacon hills and the lines of sight between them.
- [Cold Years, Witch Years?](https://tristan-hotham.github.io/witch-hunts/): Scotland's 3,840 accused witches: did the panics follow cold years? They did not.
- [Canal Mania](https://tristan-hotham.github.io/canal-mania/): Did England's canal towns boom, and do they still lead? Bigger today, not richer.
- [Cholera 1849](https://tristan-hotham.github.io/cholera-1849/): Farr's elevation law, Snow's pump and whether London's worst-hit districts are still the poorest.
- [Rotten Boroughs: Where Are They Now?](https://tristan-hotham.github.io/rotten-boroughs/): The 56 boroughs the 1832 Reform Act abolished, followed to today: 25 are villages, a hamlet or an empty hill.
- [Did Lighthouses Work?](https://tristan-hotham.github.io/did-lighthouses-work/): Did shipwrecks fall after the lights were lit?
- [Whose Ships Sank Where?](https://tristan-hotham.github.io/wrecks-of-europe/): 106,550 shipwrecks in Europe's seas, 1744 to 1945, by the flag they sailed under.
- [Thankful Villages: Luck or Size?](https://tristan-hotham.github.io/thankful-villages/): Why did every man come home to some First World War parishes?
- [Far From Home](https://tristan-hotham.github.io/victoria-cross/): Where each Victoria Cross was won, where its recipient was born, and the distance between.
- [There and Back Again](https://tristan-hotham.github.io/there-and-back-again/): 43 great journeys from 600 BC to 1947, mapped and graded by the evidence that they happened.
- [The Turnpike Towns](https://tristan-hotham.github.io/turnpike-towns/): England's 1,245 turnpike trusts and 10,927 surviving milestones and toll houses: did the towns on the turnpikes grow faster? Not that the data show.
- [What's the King on Your Postbox?](https://tristan-hotham.github.io/postbox-kings/): The royal cypher on 61,435 UK post boxes: 3,614 still carry Victoria's VR and 143 Edward VIII's.
- [The Lido Boom and Bust](https://tristan-hotham.github.io/lido-boom/): An open register of 73 British lidos: 33 of 49 dated openings came in the 1930s, and 14 closed pools have reopened.
- [Did the Pits Closing Still Show?](https://tristan-hotham.github.io/pit-closures/): 167 of 196 deep mines closed between 1984 and 1994. Forty years on, pit towns have 2.0 points more adults long-term sick than similar towns.
- [Bombs on Britain](https://tristan-hotham.github.io/air-raids/): All 61,542 civilians on the CWGC's Second World War roll for the UK, town by town: 51% died outside London.
- [Ring the Changes](https://tristan-hotham.github.io/ring-the-changes/): All 7,262 rings of change-ringing bells in Dove's Guide, mapped: jubilees, world wars and seven centuries of bell founding.
- [How Many Names on Your War Memorial?](https://tristan-hotham.github.io/war-memorials/): First World War names on 14,119 community memorials: 18.3 for every 1,000 people living there today, and only 36 small places record no dead.
- [Did the Railway Invent the Seaside?](https://tristan-hotham.github.io/seaside-railways/): Britain's resorts were already growing 1.6% a year before the railway came. The line brought a burst in its first decade.
- [Captain Swing](https://tristan-hotham.github.io/captain-swing/): 3,117 Swing riot incidents of 1830: parishes with a threshing machine were twice as likely to riot, but 90% of riotous parishes had none.
- [Britain's Last Line of Defence](https://tristan-hotham.github.io/stop-lines/): 14,690 pillboxes and defences of 1940: the planned German landing beaches had 2.3 times the average density.
- [Did Beeching Kill Your Town?](https://tristan-hotham.github.io/beeching-cuts/): 3,331 stations closed from 1955 to 1975, and 643 towns lost every one. They are a little poorer and more car-bound today, but did not fall off a cliff.
- [Great Fires](https://tristan-hotham.github.io/great-fires/): 102 great town fires, 63% of them in the 1600s and 1700s: did the towns that burned rebuild bigger? Not clearly.
- [Who Gets a Blue Plaque?](https://tristan-hotham.github.io/blue-plaques/): 6,476 plaques across the UK and all 1,037 English Heritage plaques in London: 16% honour women, but 54% of those since 2020.

**Land, sea and sky**

- [Drowned and Rising](https://tristan-hotham.github.io/sea-level/): A bathtub model of every sea level from the Ice Age to an ice-free world: Britain becomes an island at −30 m, and at +70 m a third of the UK is under water.
- [How Many Months Are Pleasantly Warm?](https://tristan-hotham.github.io/pleasant-months/): Months with afternoon highs of 18 to 27 °C: the average city dweller gets 3.6 a year now and 2.9 by 2041 to 2060. Pick your own band.
- [Sun and Rain: the World](https://tristan-hotham.github.io/sun-and-rain/): The world's sunshine against its rainfall: only 3% of land is both sunny and wet, and 84% is sunnier than anywhere in the UK.
- [Sun and Rain: the UK](https://tristan-hotham.github.io/sun-and-rain-uk/): Every square kilometre of the UK by sunshine and rainfall at once. Since 1961 to 1990 the country has become 5.6% sunnier and 7.2% wetter.
- [When Did Your Town Last See a Total Eclipse?](https://tristan-hotham.github.io/eclipse-towns/): Every path of totality across Britain and Ireland from AD 1000 to 2200, tested for 17,103 places: for most towns the last was in 1715 or 1724.
- [Britain's Earthquakes Since 1382](https://tristan-hotham.github.io/british-earthquakes/): All 7,032 quakes in the BGS catalogues: a magnitude 4 about every 3 years, and the busiest modern zones were coalfields.
- [Where Are Britain's Oldest Trees?](https://tristan-hotham.github.io/ancient-trees/): 1,626 notable trees on OpenStreetMap: oaks dominate, yews hug the old churches, and giants stand in the royal forests.
